# Exhaust the Design Space

For a novel interaction or consequential architectural choice with no useful precedent, build two or three small competing prototypes and compare them side by side before choosing. State the criteria first: the user outcome, constraints, integration cost, and the uncertainty each prototype resolves. Use throwaway artifacts rather than committing to production structure prematurely.

It also applies to product decisions where experience depends on feel. Scale the prototypes to the authorized time and scope; an established pattern or a mechanical change does not need a design competition. Report which evidence distinguishes the choices and leave product decisions the user owns open.
